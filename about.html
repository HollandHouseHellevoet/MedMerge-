We built MedMerge because the alternative was watching practices overpay forever.

Why MedMerge Exists

MedMerge was built on a simple conviction: independent medicine delivers better care at a lower cost.

The lower cost is not accidental. It is structural.

CMS itself pays differently depending on where care is delivered. Perform an appropriate treatment in a physician procedure room and the physician can receive an enhanced professional payment with no separate facility fee. Move that procedure to an ambulatory surgery center and the facility reimbursement is still lower than the hospital outpatient department. Move it into the hospital and the price rises again.

Same physician. Same procedure. Different building. Different price.

Independent medicine has the better cost structure.

Health systems have the better capital structure.

That is the problem MedMerge was built to solve.

Large health systems operate at scale. They can access tax-advantaged capital, spread risk across thousands of employees, negotiate purchasing agreements, centralize insurance programs, and build enormous balance sheets.

Independent physicians rarely have those advantages.

Traditional medical practices were built to practice medicine and distribute professional income. They were not designed to accumulate institutional capital. Money comes into the practice, expenses get paid, and earnings are distributed to the partners.

Meanwhile, the health system down the street keeps building its balance sheet.

It buys another practice.

Another building.

Another surgery center.

Another insurance product.

Another piece of the healthcare dollar.

Over time, the physician can become economically weaker even while delivering care more efficiently.

MedMerge exists to change that equation.

Turn Expenses Into Assets

Independent practices already spend enormous amounts of money on health benefits, workers' compensation, property and casualty insurance, medical malpractice, purchasing, and other operating expenses.

The question is simple:

Why should physicians finance everyone else's balance sheet instead of building their own?

MedMerge helps practices aggregate those expenses and participate in the economics they are already funding.

Self-funded health benefits allow practices to retain the economics of good plan performance rather than transferring the entire healthcare dollar to an insurance carrier.

Captive structures can allow physicians to participate in underwriting profit and investment income from risks they were already paying someone else to insure.

Direct contracting connects employers and patients directly with independent physicians instead of automatically routing healthcare dollars through hospital-controlled networks.

Purchasing at scale gives independent practices access to economics historically reserved for much larger organizations.

The objective is not merely to reduce expenses.

It is to convert expenses into capital.

That is the fundamental idea behind MedMerge.

Scale Without Consolidation

For decades, physicians have been told that the only way to obtain scale is to surrender independence.

Sell the practice.

Join the hospital.

Take private equity.

Become an employee.

We reject that premise.

Physicians need the advantages of scale. They do not need to give up ownership to get them.

MedMerge gives independent physician practices the financial infrastructure of a health system without requiring them to become one.

Keep the practice.

Keep the autonomy.

Keep the equity.

Aggregate everything else.

Every practice that joins strengthens the economics of the practices already inside the network. More lives create purchasing power. More physicians create contracting power. More premium and risk create better opportunities for capital formation.

The network becomes stronger without requiring the individual physician to become less independent.

That is the model.

Independence. Amplified.

Independent physicians are not losing because they practice inferior medicine.

They are losing because the organizations acquiring them have spent decades building financial infrastructure around healthcare while physicians focused on delivering healthcare.

We can fix that.

The physicians who choose independence deserve the same economic tools available to the institutions trying to acquire them.

Better benefits. Better risk structures. Better purchasing. Direct contracts. Capital formation.

The benefits of scale without surrendering ownership.

That is why MedMerge exists.
